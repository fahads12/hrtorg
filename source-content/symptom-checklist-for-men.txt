Your test scores indicate a possible hormone imbalance, which can cause multiple symptoms and could lead to serious health consequences that may require medical intervention or treatment.
Hormones work to control nearly every process in a woman’s body including metabolic, cellular, and reproductive health, and need to be balanced for proper function.
Hormones are not only necessary for reproductive function, but also help control and aid the body in energy, weight, mood, and heart rate as well.
As women age, hormone levels fluctuate in response to the body’s demand for reproductive function whether or not a woman becomes pregnant. When a young woman reaches puberty and the beginning of menses, hormone levels may create symptoms associated with premenstrual syndrome (PMS) such as low mood, acne, headaches, and poor cognitive function. Throughout the reproductive years, hormones control fertility and support pregnancy as well. Once women reach middle age, the fluctuation and reduction of certain hormones trigger the menopause transition and accompanying symptoms.
