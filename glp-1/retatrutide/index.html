Retatrutide — an investigational triple-receptor agonist.

An experimental weight-loss drug from Eli Lilly that activates three receptors (GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon). Currently in Phase 3 trials and not yet available by prescription.

Educational page only

Retatrutide is not FDA-approved and is not prescribed by HRT.org. It is an investigational drug currently in Phase 3 clinical trials. There is no legally compounded version available in the U.S., and anything marketed as "retatrutide" outside a clinical trial is not from the manufacturer and is not something our clinicians prescribe. This page exists so patients searching for retatrutide can understand what it is and what to do while it is still in development.

How retatrutide works

Retatrutide is a once-weekly injectable that acts as an agonist at three different hormone receptors at once:

  • GLP-1 receptor — slows gastric emptying, lowers glucose, and reduces appetite (the mechanism behind semaglutide and tirzepatide).
  • GIP receptor — a second incretin receptor that improves insulin response (the mechanism tirzepatide added to GLP-1).
  • Glucagon receptor — unique to retatrutide in this class, glucagon activation is thought to increase energy expenditure and lipolysis.

In theory, adding glucagon activation on top of the dual-incretin mechanism should drive more fat loss per unit of appetite suppression. Early-phase trial data appears to support this, though Phase 3 outcomes and long-term safety are still being collected.

What the trial data shows

The Phase 2 trial published in The New England Journal of Medicine in 2023 reported mean weight loss of approximately 24% at the highest dose (12 mg once weekly) after 48 weeks in adults with obesity — the largest effect size reported for any weight-loss drug to date in a Phase 2 setting. By comparison:

  • Semaglutide 2.4 mg: ~15% mean weight loss at 68 weeks (STEP 1).
  • Tirzepatide 15 mg: ~20–22% mean weight loss at 72 weeks (SURMOUNT-1).
  • Retatrutide 12 mg: ~24% at 48 weeks (Phase 2) — but with a smaller trial population and shorter follow-up.

These numbers are not directly comparable — different trials, different populations, different durations. Phase 3 results from the TRIUMPH program will be the deciding evidence.

Where it is in development

Eli Lilly's Phase 3 program (TRIUMPH) is underway and covers obesity, obesity with cardiovascular risk, obesity with type 2 diabetes, obstructive sleep apnea, and other indications. Readouts and FDA filing timing are company-controlled and have shifted during development; based on currently announced timelines, FDA review would be no earlier than 2026 and more likely 2026–2027 if the trials are positive.

Retatrutide is not on the FDA drug shortage list (which is the regulatory pathway that enabled compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide). Compounding pharmacies cannot legally source API for a drug that is still in Phase 3 clinical development, so any product sold online as "retatrutide" is not from Eli Lilly and is not something a licensed U.S. clinician will prescribe.

Side effects reported in trials

Reported in Phase 2 — these may change in Phase 3 and the drug's final label may look different:

  • GI side effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea) — the most common adverse events, generally mild-to-moderate and dose-dependent, consistent with other GLP-1 class drugs.
  • Modest heart-rate increase (a few beats per minute on average).
  • Small transient increases in liver enzymes and blood pressure in some participants.
  • Potential concerns being evaluated: thyroid C-cell tumors (a class-wide watch), gallbladder events, pancreatitis.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy retatrutide from a compounding pharmacy?

No. Retatrutide is still in Phase 3 clinical trials and is not on the FDA drug shortage list, which is the regulatory pathway that enabled compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide. Compounding pharmacies cannot legally source retatrutide API from the manufacturer. Products sold online as "retatrutide" are not sourced from Eli Lilly, and quality, identity, and sterility cannot be verified.

When will retatrutide be FDA-approved?

That is up to Eli Lilly's trial readouts and the FDA's review. Based on currently announced Phase 3 timelines, approval would be no earlier than 2026 and more likely 2026–2027 if the pivotal trials meet their endpoints.

Will it really deliver 24% weight loss in the real world?

The 24% figure comes from a Phase 2 trial with a smaller, selected population — it is a signal, not a label claim. Real-world effect sizes for every GLP-1 class drug have been somewhat lower than their clinical trials, driven by adherence, dose titration, and lifestyle factors. Phase 3 readouts will be more representative.

Can I join a retatrutide clinical trial?

Possibly. Current and upcoming trials are listed on clinicaltrials.gov (search "retatrutide"). HRT.org does not operate clinical trial sites.

Will HRT.org offer retatrutide once it's approved?

Our weight-loss program covers the FDA-approved GLP-1 class. If retatrutide reaches approval, we will evaluate adding it the same way we evaluated adding semaglutide and tirzepatide. This page will be updated at that time.
