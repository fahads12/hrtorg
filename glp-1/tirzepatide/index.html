Tirzepatide — the dual-receptor GLP-1.

A newer, often more effective option that activates both GLP-1 and GIP pathways.

Overview

Tirzepatide is a first-in-class dual agonist — it activates two gut hormone receptors (GLP-1 and GIP) with a single molecule, making it mechanistically distinct from semaglutide and other first-generation GLP-1 drugs. FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes as Mounjaro (2022) and chronic weight management as Zepbound (2023), tirzepatide has produced the largest weight-loss effects of any approved pharmacologic agent in clinical trials to date.

How tirzepatide works

Where semaglutide targets only the GLP-1 receptor, tirzepatide binds both GLP-1 and GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide) receptors with high affinity. GIP works alongside GLP-1 on insulin secretion, lipid handling, and appetite regulation. Hitting both pathways simultaneously appears to produce a greater metabolic and weight-loss effect than engaging GLP-1 alone.

Mechanisms layered on top of GLP-1's standard actions:

  • Enhanced insulin sensitization — GIP amplifies glucose-dependent insulin release.
  • Improved lipid metabolism — stronger reductions in triglycerides and LDL than semaglutide in head-to-head trials.
  • Different side-effect profile — some patients tolerate tirzepatide better; some tolerate semaglutide better. The receptor interaction is genuinely different.
  • Greater weight-loss ceiling — in SURMOUNT-1 (72 weeks, non-diabetic adults with obesity), the highest dose (15 mg) produced mean weight loss of 20.9%, compared with ~15% typically seen with semaglutide 2.4 mg.

Who tirzepatide is for

Zepbound's FDA labeling matches semaglutide: BMI ≥ 30, or BMI ≥ 27 with a weight-related comorbidity. Mounjaro is labeled specifically for type 2 diabetes.

Clinically, tirzepatide is often the better choice when:

  • You've been a non-responder or partial responder on semaglutide — hitting GIP in addition to GLP-1 opens a new pharmacological lever.
  • Metabolic improvement is a primary goal (HbA1c, triglycerides, insulin resistance).
  • You have higher starting BMI and need larger absolute weight loss.
  • Your goal weight represents >15% body-weight reduction, where tirzepatide's ceiling is meaningfully higher.

Same contraindications as semaglutide: MTC/MEN2 history, pancreatitis history, pregnancy/breastfeeding, severe gastroparesis, type 1 diabetes.

What to expect — results and timeline

Tirzepatide titrates monthly, starting at 2.5 mg once weekly and increasing to a maintenance dose of 5 mg, 10 mg, or 15 mg based on response and tolerability.

SURMOUNT-1 trial results at 72 weeks (non-diabetic adults):

  • 5 mg weekly: mean weight loss 15.0%
  • 10 mg weekly: mean weight loss 19.5%
  • 15 mg weekly: mean weight loss 20.9%
  • Placebo: 3.1%

Roughly 56% of patients on the highest dose achieved ≥20% weight loss — a magnitude previously only possible with bariatric surgery.

Monthly expectations are similar to semaglutide early on, but the trajectory tends to continue further rather than plateau at month 6. Full effect typically settles by month 10–14.

Metabolic markers improve in parallel: HbA1c typically drops 1.5–2.5 points in diabetic patients; lipid profiles improve; blood pressure often falls modestly.

Side effects and safety

Side-effect profile is very similar to semaglutide — unsurprising since both act through GLP-1. The main differences in head-to-head experience:

Nausea is reported slightly more often early on with tirzepatide, likely because of the higher potency and faster effect. Titration helps.

Constipation and diarrhea are both common, often alternating. Fiber, hydration, and a magnesium supplement help many patients.

Injection-site reactions are mostly mild and transient.

Hypoglycemia risk is low on tirzepatide alone but increases if you're also on insulin or sulfonylureas — those typically need dose reduction.

Boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors is the same as semaglutide and based on the same preclinical data; MTC/MEN2 is an absolute contraindication.

Rare serious events include pancreatitis, severe allergic reactions, and gallbladder disease. Call your provider for severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, or signs of allergic reaction.

Dosing and protocol

Standard tirzepatide titration:

  • Weeks 1–4: 2.5 mg once weekly
  • Weeks 5–8: 5 mg once weekly (first therapeutic dose)
  • Weeks 9–12: 7.5 mg once weekly
  • Weeks 13–16: 10 mg once weekly
  • Weeks 17–20: 12.5 mg once weekly
  • Week 21+: 15 mg once weekly (maximum)

In practice, many patients hit their goal somewhere between 5 and 10 mg and stay there as a maintenance dose. Pushing to 15 mg is usually reserved for patients who need maximum effect or who've plateaued at lower doses.

Compounded tirzepatide dispensed by HRT.org uses the same titration schedule as the branded product. Your provider adjusts based on your response and tolerability.

Frequently asked questions

Is tirzepatide better than semaglutide?

In head-to-head trials for weight loss, tirzepatide produces greater average weight loss. But "better" depends on the patient — tolerability, cost, insurance, and prior response all matter. We often start patients on semaglutide and switch to tirzepatide if response is inadequate, or we start with tirzepatide when goals are aggressive or when semaglutide has already failed.

What's the difference between Mounjaro and Zepbound?

They're the same molecule — tirzepatide — just marketed under different brand names and FDA indications. Mounjaro is labeled for type 2 diabetes; Zepbound is labeled for chronic weight management. Insurance coverage, pricing, and co-pay programs differ between the two. Compounded tirzepatide is a separate category from either.

Will I regain weight if I stop?

The SURMOUNT-4 trial showed significant weight regain within one year of discontinuation without active maintenance. Our protocol includes a maintenance phase at lower dose, plus lifestyle structure, strength training, and periodic lab monitoring to preserve as much benefit as possible if you choose to come off.

Does insurance cover tirzepatide?

Insurance coverage for GLP-1s remains variable. Diabetic indication (Mounjaro) is more often covered. Weight-management indication (Zepbound) coverage expanded in 2024 but is still inconsistent. HRT.org dispenses compounded tirzepatide, which is cash-pay but typically HSA/FSA eligible and significantly less expensive than brand.
