Overview

About HRT for women

As women age, sex steroid hormone levels decline — causing hot flashes, night sweats, mood changes, sleep disruption, brain fog, vaginal dryness, and weight gain. Hormone replacement therapy restores balance with bioidentical estradiol and progesterone, delivered transdermally for the best modern safety profile.

Vasomotor symptoms (hot flashes, night sweats) alone affect roughly 75% of women during the menopausal transition — and HRT resolves them for 90% within 2–8 weeks.

Benefits

What patients experience on HRT.

Fewer hot flashes and night sweats

HRT regulates estrogen and progesterone levels, reducing the frequency and severity of vasomotor symptoms for most women within 4–8 weeks.

Better sleep

Bioidentical progesterone at bedtime shortens sleep latency and reduces 3 AM wake-ups. Hot flash control also protects sleep continuity.

Stabilized mood

Estrogen stabilizes serotonin; progesterone works via GABA. Together they often resolve perimenopausal anxiety and mood swings more cleanly than SSRIs alone.

Increased libido

Restoring estrogen and (where indicated) low-dose testosterone can meaningfully improve sexual desire.

Greater bone strength

Estrogen is one of the strongest known interventions to prevent postmenopausal bone loss and reduce fracture risk.

Vaginal health and GSM relief

Local vaginal estrogen resolves dryness, painful intercourse, and recurrent UTIs with minimal systemic absorption.

Improved cognition

Many women report improved clarity and reduced brain fog within 2–3 months of starting transdermal estradiol.

Stabilized weight

HRT can protect against the central fat redistribution typical of estrogen loss, especially combined with strength training.

Greater energy

Balanced hormones result in increased vitality — a common and reliable response.

FAQ

Common questions.

Is HRT safe for women?

Modern HRT with transdermal estradiol and bioidentical progesterone, started within 10 years of menopause, has a favorable benefit-risk profile for most women. The 2002 WHI concerns applied to a specific regimen (oral conjugated estrogens + synthetic progestins in older women) that modern practice has largely moved past.

What are the side effects?

Common early side effects include breast tenderness, mild bloating, and (in continuous combined regimens) occasional breakthrough bleeding in the first 6 months. Serious side effects are rare on modern transdermal protocols. Your provider reviews your personal history for any specific risk factors.

What is bioidentical HRT?

Bioidentical hormones are molecularly identical to what your body makes. FDA-approved bioidenticals (estradiol patch, oral micronized progesterone) are the standard. Compounded bioidenticals offer flexible dosing. Both can be clinically appropriate — your provider helps you choose.

How do I know if I need HRT?

Classic indicators: hot flashes, night sweats, cycle irregularity, sleep disruption, mood changes, vaginal dryness, and brain fog — especially if they coincide with your mid-40s to mid-50s. Our 2-minute quiz helps clarify whether HRT is likely a fit.
