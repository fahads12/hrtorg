The research view

Mood doesn't exist independently of your endocrine system. Serotonin synthesis depends on estradiol; GABA depends on progesterone; dopamine responds to thyroid state. When mood changes track with your cycle, pregnancy, or peri/menopause, the hormone layer is usually part of the picture.

Why it happens

The hormone connection to mood changes.

Neurotransmitter balance isn't independent — it rides on the hormonal substrate. These four show up most often.

01

Estrogen fluctuation

Perimenopausal estrogen swings destabilize serotonin. Stabilizing estrogen often stabilizes mood more cleanly than SSRIs alone.

02

Progesterone drop

Progesterone is calming via GABA. Low or falling progesterone shows up as anxiety and sleep-onset issues.

03

Thyroid dysfunction

Both hypo and hyper present as mood changes. Hypo looks like depression; hyper like anxiety.

04

Cortisol dysregulation

Chronic stress rewires the HPA axis. Diurnal cortisol testing shows the pattern — and the pattern dictates the treatment.

Common questions

About mood changes.

How do I know if I need HRT vs. an SSRI?

Not either/or. If mood tracks closely with your cycle or started in perimenopause, hormone intervention often works better or in combination. For moderate-to-severe depression, we refer to a psychiatrist alongside hormone care.

Will HRT make my anxiety worse?

Rarely. Progesterone is typically calming. Estradiol stabilizes serotonin. Rare patients are sensitive and need dose adjustments — we titrate carefully and follow up.

Can hormone imbalance cause depression on its own?

Yes. Postpartum, perimenopausal, and thyroid-driven depression are all well-documented and often resolve with hormone intervention.

If I'm already on an SSRI, do I need to stop before HRT?

No. Many patients benefit from both. Your provider coordinates with your prescribing physician.
