Concern

Desire is a vital sign. And it's hormonal.

Men and women both lose desire when testosterone, DHEA, or estrogen fall off. The good news: all three are testable and treatable. The better news: when you fix the hormone layer, mood and energy usually follow.

The research view

Libido is often the first symptom people notice but the last one they talk about. In both sexes, free testosterone, estradiol, DHEA, and thyroid state all influence desire and arousal. The workup is identical regardless of gender; the protocol differs.

Why it happens

The hormone connection to low libido.

Libido depends on multiple hormones in both sexes. Here's where to start.

01

Low testosterone (all genders)

Men below ~450 ng/dL and women with low free T often report flat libido. Replacement protocols differ by sex, but both are evidence-based.

02

Estrogen decline (women)

Vaginal dryness, pain with intercourse, and reduced arousal often resolve with topical or systemic estrogen.

03

DHEA deficiency

DHEA-S is a precursor to testosterone and estrogen. Low levels — common after 50 — can be supplemented safely under provider guidance.

04

Thyroid & cortisol

Both affect libido indirectly through energy and mood. The full workup includes them.

Common questions

About low libido.

Can women safely use testosterone?

Yes, in low doses. Testosterone therapy for women is off-label in the US but has strong evidence for libido in postmenopausal women. We prescribe compounded low-dose cream under careful monitoring.

Will testosterone help with erectile dysfunction?

If T is low, often yes. If T is normal, we evaluate for vascular, neurologic, or psychological causes and may add tadalafil or sildenafil.

Is this covered by insurance?

We don't bill insurance directly. HRT and TRT are often reimbursable out-of-network. HSA/FSA eligible.

How discreet is the delivery?

Prescriptions ship in unmarked packaging from licensed compounding pharmacies. All communication is through the secure patient portal.
