Concern

Sleep that doesn't restore you is a signal, not a lifestyle problem.

Waking at 3 AM, or feeling exhausted after 8 hours of sleep, can be related to hormones. Progesterone, melatonin, cortisol rhythms, and thyroid function all affect sleep. We look for the pattern and work to address it.

The research view

Sleep depends on several systems, and chronic insomnia is rarely about melatonin alone. Before recommending treatment, we assess your daily cortisol pattern, changes in progesterone and estrogen, and thyroid activity.

Why it happens

The hormone connection to poor sleep.

Sleep is governed by a cascade. When one hormone falls out of rhythm, the whole night suffers.

01

Low progesterone

For women in perimenopause and menopause, falling progesterone removes a natural GABA-ergic sedative. 3 AM wake-ups are a classic signal.

02

Elevated night cortisol

Chronic stress or late-day caffeine keeps cortisol high when it should be low. Diurnal cortisol testing (× 4 samples) shows the actual curve.

03

Thyroid overactivity

High-normal thyroid can present as racing thoughts and poor sleep. We check free T3, free T4, and TPOab — not just TSH.

04

Melatonin & circadian signaling

Blue light, inconsistent schedules, and age all suppress endogenous melatonin. Supplementation plus a sleep hygiene plan is often the first lever.

Common questions

About poor sleep.

I take melatonin and still wake at 3 AM. What am I missing?

Melatonin helps with sleep onset, not maintenance. 3 AM wake-ups are most often progesterone-related (in women) or cortisol-related (in both sexes). Testing tells you which.

Is bioidentical progesterone safe long-term?

Oral micronized progesterone (prescription) is well-studied, including for post-menopausal women on estrogen. Your provider reviews risks, benefits, and alternatives before any prescription.

Could my thyroid be causing insomnia even if TSH is normal?

Yes. High-normal TSH with low free T3, or elevated TPOab, can disrupt sleep. We test the full panel — not just TSH alone.

What if labs come back normal?

Then we focus on sleep habits, light exposure, caffeine timing, and stress management. Factors beyond hormones can affect sleep too.
