Concern

Hot flashes aren't inevitable. They're treatable.

Vasomotor symptoms — hot flashes, night sweats, flushing — are the most common and treatable sign of estrogen decline. Hormone therapy resolves them for most women. We also offer non-hormonal options when HRT isn't right for you.

The research view

Vasomotor symptoms are the one menopausal complaint with the clearest, best-studied treatment. Transdermal estradiol with cyclic or continuous progesterone resolves hot flashes for 90%+ of women within 2–8 weeks. Modern evidence is clear on safety when started within the menopausal transition.

Why it happens

The hormone connection to hot flashes.

The vasomotor system is directly regulated by estrogen. When estrogen drops, the thermoregulatory window narrows.

01

Estrogen decline

The hypothalamic thermostat loses buffering as estradiol falls. HRT restores the buffer in most cases within weeks.

02

Progesterone imbalance

Progesterone has calming and cooling effects. In perimenopause, erratic progesterone can worsen night sweats.

03

Cortisol & stress

High cortisol narrows the thermoregulatory window further. Stress management + testing cortisol rhythm often helps.

04

Thyroid overactivity

Hyperthyroidism can mimic or amplify hot flashes. We rule it out in every workup.

Treatment options

What we may prescribe

HRT is first-line and most effective. Non-hormonal options exist for women who can't or don't want HRT.

  • Transdermal estradiolpatch, 0.025–0.1mg
  • Oral micronized progesterone100–200mg night

  • SSRI (if HRT contraindicated)paroxetine, venlafaxine

Common questions

About hot flashes.

Is HRT safe?

For most women starting within 10 years of menopause, modern evidence supports HRT with a favorable benefit-risk profile. Your provider reviews your personal and family history, including breast cancer and clotting risk.

How long until hot flashes improve?

Most women see substantial relief within 2–8 weeks of starting estradiol. Full response typically by 3 months.

Can I stop HRT once hot flashes improve?

Some women do, after a few years. We taper gradually. Many choose to continue for ongoing bone, cardiovascular, and cognitive support — a decision we revisit annually.

What if I had breast cancer?

Standard HRT is contraindicated. We offer non-hormonal protocols (SSRI, gabapentin, lifestyle) that reduce hot flash frequency and severity meaningfully.
