Sermorelin — natural GH stimulation.

A GHRH analog that works with your body's existing axis rather than replacing it.

Overview

Sermorelin is a synthetic analog of growth-hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH), the natural hypothalamic peptide that signals the pituitary to release growth hormone (GH). Unlike direct recombinant GH injections, sermorelin preserves the body's natural pulsatile GH release pattern — including the critical nighttime pulse that drives most of GH's restorative effects. It's one of the most-studied and clinically useful peptides in the GH-secretagogue category.

How sermorelin works

Growth hormone is released from the pituitary in pulses throughout the day, with the largest pulse occurring during stage 3 deep sleep. GH production peaks in adolescence and declines steadily through adulthood — by age 60, most adults produce 30–50% of their youthful GH output.

Low GH contributes to loss of lean body mass, increased visceral fat, poor sleep quality, slower recovery from training, thinner skin, and reduced resilience. The direct GH approach (daily recombinant GH injections) carries safety concerns at supraphysiological doses — it suppresses the natural axis entirely and can promote tissue overgrowth.

Sermorelin takes a different approach. By binding GHRH receptors on the pituitary, it stimulates endogenous GH release while preserving the pituitary feedback loops that prevent overproduction. The result: a restoration of youthful GH pulsatility rather than steady saturation.

Sermorelin is often combined with GHRPs (growth-hormone-releasing peptides) like ipamorelin for a synergistic effect — the two pathways amplify each other, and the combined stack typically produces more consistent GH response than either alone.

Who sermorelin is for

Sermorelin is used in adults seeking to support:

  • Recovery from exercise and training stress
  • Body composition (modest reduction in visceral fat, modest preservation of lean mass)
  • Sleep quality — particularly stage 3 deep sleep
  • Skin elasticity and connective tissue
  • General resilience and energy

Candidates typically have IGF-1 levels in the lower half of the normal range for their age, mild symptoms of GH decline, and no contraindications. Lab assessment (IGF-1 as the primary GH surrogate) guides starting decisions and monitoring.

Not appropriate for:

  • Active malignancy (GH can theoretically promote tumor growth)
  • Pregnancy or active trying to conceive
  • Severe untreated obstructive sleep apnea
  • Hypersensitivity to GHRH analogs
  • Children with closed growth plates (sermorelin is approved for pediatric growth deficiency; adult use is off-label)

What to expect

Response unfolds gradually over months:

  • Weeks 2–4: sleep quality often improves first. Patients report deeper sleep and feeling more rested.
  • Months 2–3: recovery from workouts improves. Reduced DOMS, better response to training.
  • Months 3–6: body composition shifts — modest reduction in visceral fat, preservation or small increase in lean mass with training.
  • Months 6+: steady-state benefits; this is where we re-evaluate labs and protocol.

Sermorelin is not a dramatic transformation drug. Effects are subtle but meaningful — most patients describe it as "feeling more like themselves" rather than a sudden change. IGF-1 levels typically rise 20–50% from baseline over the first 3–6 months.

Side effects and safety

Sermorelin has an excellent safety profile when used appropriately. The most common side effects:

  • Injection site reactions (redness, mild swelling) — common, usually self-limiting
  • Flushing or warmth right after injection — brief, usually within 15 minutes
  • Mild headache — uncommon
  • Vivid dreams — not uncommon in the first few weeks

Because sermorelin stimulates endogenous GH rather than replacing it, the pituitary's natural feedback mechanisms prevent GH overproduction. This is a meaningful safety advantage over direct recombinant GH use.

Rare but possible: hypothyroidism (secondary to altered pituitary signaling — we monitor TSH), insulin resistance (uncommon at therapeutic doses), fluid retention at high doses.

Monitoring: IGF-1 at baseline, 90 days, and then every 6 months. TSH and metabolic panel at baseline and annually.

Dosing

Standard protocol:

  • Dose: 200–500 mcg subcutaneously once daily
  • Timing: at bedtime on an empty stomach — mimics the natural nighttime GH pulse and amplifies it
  • Duration: typically run in 3–6 month cycles with periodic breaks, though continuous use is also common

Often combined with ipamorelin (100–200 mcg at the same injection) for a GHRH + GHRP stack. The combination typically produces stronger IGF-1 response than sermorelin alone.

Compounded sermorelin dispensed by HRT.org comes as a reconstituted vial with calibrated syringes. Your provider walks you through the first injection, dosing, and timing.

Frequently asked questions

How is sermorelin different from HGH injections?

Recombinant HGH (synthetic growth hormone) replaces your body's GH directly, which suppresses your natural production and can cause overproduction effects. Sermorelin stimulates your pituitary to release its own GH — preserving the natural feedback loops and pulsatility. Safer profile, more sustainable.

Is sermorelin legal?

Yes — sermorelin is FDA-approved for pediatric growth hormone deficiency and prescribed off-label for adult use. It's dispensed through licensed compounding pharmacies under a prescription from a licensed physician.

When will I notice a difference?

Sleep improvements often come first, usually within 2–4 weeks. Recovery and body composition changes develop over 2–3 months. The full benefit of a sermorelin protocol typically settles around month 4–6, which is when we re-check IGF-1 and adjust.

Should I combine sermorelin with ipamorelin?

For most patients, yes. The two peptides work through different receptors (GHRH vs. GHRP) and the combination produces a stronger, more consistent GH pulse than either alone. Many compounded formulations contain both in a single injection for convenience.
