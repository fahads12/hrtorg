What it is

What is hormone replacement therapy?

Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) restores estradiol, progesterone, and testosterone to physiologic ranges to treat the symptoms of menopause, perimenopause, andropause, and clinically diagnosed hormone deficiency.

When ovarian or testicular hormone production declines — through natural aging, surgical removal, chemotherapy, or primary endocrine failure — the result is a predictable cascade: hot flashes, sleep disruption, mood changes, cognitive slowing, muscle loss, bone loss, sexual dysfunction, and reduced metabolic resilience. HRT replaces the hormones your body is no longer making, in doses matched to your labs and symptoms.

Modern HRT uses bioidentical hormones — molecules structurally identical to what your body produces — delivered through transdermal patches, creams, oral capsules, intramuscular injection, or subcutaneous pellets. The right route, dose, and timing depend on your sex, age, symptoms, and goals.

The three hormones
  • Estradiol (E2)The dominant estrogen for premenopausal women. Protects bones, blood vessels, cognition, skin, and mood. Replaced in menopause with transdermal patches, creams, or oral tablets.
  • ProgesteroneThe counter-balance to estrogen. Protects the uterine lining, stabilizes mood, supports sleep. Prescribed as oral micronized progesterone for women with a uterus on estrogen.
  • Testosterone (T)Dominant in men, important in women. Drives libido, muscle mass, cognition, and energy. Delivered as injection, cream, or pellet for men; low-dose cream for women.
For Women

Hormone replacement therapy for women

HRT is the most effective evidence-based treatment for the symptoms of perimenopause and menopause. For most healthy women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause, HRT resolves vasomotor symptoms, protects bone density, reduces cardiovascular risk, and supports cognitive and sexual health. We prescribe both FDA-approved and compounded bioidentical options.

What we treat
  • Hot flashes and night sweats (vasomotor symptoms)
  • Vaginal dryness, painful intercourse, recurrent UTIs (genitourinary syndrome of menopause)
  • Sleep disruption and insomnia
  • Mood changes, anxiety, perimenopausal depression
  • Low libido and sexual dysfunction
  • Bone loss and osteoporosis prevention
  • Perimenopausal cycle irregularity and heavy bleeding
  • Cognitive changes and brain fog
How we prescribe
  • Transdermal estradiol — patches, gel, or cream. First-line for most women. Avoids first-pass liver metabolism and the clotting risk of oral estrogen.
  • Oral micronized progesterone — Prometrium or compounded. Required for women with a uterus on systemic estrogen; also used for sleep and anxiety.
  • Low-dose testosterone cream — compounded. Added when libido, energy, and muscle changes don't resolve with estrogen alone.
  • Vaginal estrogen — low-dose cream, tablet, or ring. For genitourinary symptoms. Minimal systemic absorption; appropriate even for women who can't take systemic HRT.

For Men

Hormone replacement therapy for men

Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) is the gold-standard treatment for clinically confirmed hypogonadism and symptomatic andropause. We diagnose with the full panel — total T, free T, SHBG, estradiol, LH, FSH, PSA, and hematocrit — then prescribe the right route and dose for your biology and goals. Fertility-safe protocols available for men who want to preserve sperm production.

What we treat
  • Low energy, fatigue, and reduced exercise capacity
  • Low libido and erectile dysfunction
  • Loss of muscle mass and strength
  • Poor recovery from training
  • Depressed mood, irritability, and cognitive slowing
  • Visceral fat gain and metabolic resistance
  • Bone loss (osteopenia and osteoporosis)
  • Hot flashes from treated prostate cancer or surgical castration
How we prescribe
  • Testosterone cypionate injection — intramuscular or subcutaneous. Weekly or twice-weekly. Most common protocol; stable levels when properly dosed.
  • Testosterone cream — compounded transdermal. Daily application. Useful for men sensitive to injection peaks.
  • Testosterone pellets — subcutaneous insertion every 3–4 months. Set-and-forget convenience.
  • Clomiphene or enclomiphene — fertility-preserving. Stimulates endogenous testosterone production without suppressing sperm. First-line for men who want children.
  • hCG — maintains testicular function and fertility alongside TRT.

The medications

Bioidentical hormone replacement therapy, FDA-approved and compounded.

We prescribe both FDA-approved commercial preparations and compounded bioidentical hormones when a specific dose or combination isn't available commercially. All medications are dispensed by licensed pharmacies.

Estrogen

Transdermal estradiol

Vivelle-Dot patches, Divigel, Estrogel, Climara, or compounded cream. Delivers estradiol through the skin, bypassing the liver.

Progestogen

Oral micronized progesterone

Prometrium or compounded. Bioidentical. Protects the uterine lining and improves sleep.

Testosterone (women)

Compounded testosterone cream

Low-dose topical. Added when libido, energy, or muscle changes persist on estrogen alone.

Testosterone (men)

Testosterone cypionate injection

Depo-Testosterone or compounded. Most common TRT protocol. Weekly or twice-weekly dosing.

Testosterone (men)

Testosterone cream

Compounded transdermal. Daily application for stable, non-fluctuating levels.

Fertility-safe

Clomiphene / enclomiphene

Clomid, Androxal. Stimulates endogenous testosterone while preserving sperm production. First-line when fertility matters.

Vaginal

Vaginal estrogen

Estrace cream, Vagifem tablet, Estring ring. Local treatment for GSM. Minimal systemic absorption.

Adjunct

DHEA, pregnenolone, thyroid support

Added when adrenal or thyroid axis co-contributes to symptoms. Prescribed only when labs support use.

Frequently asked questions

HRT questions, answered.

The questions patients ask most often. More specific concerns go to your provider during the consultation.

Is hormone replacement therapy safe?
For most healthy women within 10 years of menopause and for men with clinically low testosterone, hormone replacement therapy is safe and effective. The Women's Health Initiative reanalyses, the North American Menopause Society position statement, and current urology guidelines all support HRT as appropriate therapy when prescribed and monitored correctly. Risk-benefit assessment is individualized by your provider — history of hormone-sensitive cancer, clotting disorder, uncontrolled cardiovascular disease, or active liver disease shape the decision.
How much does hormone replacement therapy cost?
HRT.org consultations start at $149 and most monthly medication costs range from $35 to $150 depending on the protocol. HSA and FSA accounts are accepted, and many patients pay with a care-credit option. We publish all pricing on each product page — no hidden fees.
Does insurance cover hormone replacement therapy?
Many commercial insurance plans cover HRT labs and medications when billed through a traditional pharmacy, though compounded bioidentical preparations are typically paid out-of-pocket. We can provide itemized receipts for submission to your plan. HSA and FSA funds apply to both consultations and medications.
What is bioidentical hormone replacement therapy?
Bioidentical hormones are molecularly identical to the hormones your body produces — the same estradiol, progesterone, and testosterone, just manufactured in a compounding pharmacy or FDA-approved commercial pharmacy. The term "bioidentical" refers only to molecular structure, not source. We prescribe both FDA-approved bioidentical products (patches, oral progesterone, testosterone cream) and compounded preparations when a specific dose or combination isn't available commercially.
How long does it take to feel the effects of HRT?
For women, hot flashes and sleep improvements usually begin within 2–4 weeks; mood and libido often take 6–12 weeks. For men on TRT, energy and libido typically improve within 3–6 weeks; muscle mass and body composition changes become visible around 12–16 weeks. We re-check labs at 6–8 weeks to confirm the dose is working.
Does TRT cause infertility?
Exogenous testosterone suppresses luteinizing hormone and sperm production — this is reversible for most men but can take 3–12 months to recover. If you want to preserve fertility while on treatment, we use clomiphene or hCG-maintained TRT instead of standard testosterone. See our TRT-safe fertility page for the clinical detail.
Can I take HRT if I've had breast cancer?
Historically HRT was considered contraindicated after breast cancer, but current oncology-endocrinology guidelines allow individualized decisions — especially for severe quality-of-life symptoms, tumor-receptor status, and time since remission. We coordinate with your oncologist and can prescribe non-hormonal alternatives (fezolinetant, SSRIs, gabapentin) when systemic estrogen isn't appropriate.
When should I start hormone replacement therapy?
For women, the "critical window" is within 10 years of your last period or before age 60 — starting during this window provides the strongest cardiovascular, cognitive, and bone-protective benefits. For men, we treat when symptoms match confirmed low testosterone, regardless of age, provided the clinical picture supports it.
