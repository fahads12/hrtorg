Overview

Insulin resistance is the hidden driver behind most hormonal, cardiovascular, and inflammatory disease — PCOS, perimenopausal weight gain, erectile dysfunction, fatty liver, early coronary disease, cognitive decline.

HbA1c and fasting glucose catch diabetes once it exists. They miss the preceding 10–20 years of compensated hyperinsulinemia, when the pancreas is still keeping glucose normal by producing more and more insulin. For early detection we add fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, and C-peptide.

A fasting insulin above 7 µIU/mL is often the earliest signal we have of impending metabolic trouble — years before HbA1c rises.

FAQ

Common questions.

Why is my HbA1c normal but I'm told I'm insulin resistant?
Because insulin resistance precedes glucose dysregulation by years. Your pancreas is still compensating — producing enough insulin to keep glucose in range. A fasting insulin above 7 µIU/mL is the early warning.
Is a 100 fasting glucose really a problem?
It's the lab-reference top of normal, but studies show cardiovascular risk starts climbing from 85 mg/dL upward. We flag anything over 90 for closer workup.
How do I interpret HOMA-IR?
HOMA-IR = (fasting insulin × fasting glucose) / 405. Values under 1.5 are optimal, 1.5–2.0 is early resistance, over 2.0 is clinically insulin resistant.
