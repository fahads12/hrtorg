Overview

A sex-hormone panel captures the current state of the HPG (hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal) axis. It is the single most useful screen for perimenopause, menopause, andropause, low libido, infertility, PCOS, and gender-affirming care monitoring.

Serum draws are the clinical standard; saliva is sometimes used to capture bioavailable fractions or to collect at home. Timing matters — estradiol and progesterone move across the menstrual cycle, so women's panels are drawn relative to cycle day (follicular Day 3 for FSH/LH/E2, luteal Day 19–21 for progesterone). Men's testosterone is drawn fasting, between 7–10am.

Interpretation is contextual: a "normal" number for a 25-year-old is often inadequate for a 55-year-old with symptoms. We read the panel together with your symptoms, binding globulins (SHBG), and metabolic context (insulin, thyroid).

FAQ

Common questions.

Why saliva instead of blood for sex hormones?
Saliva reflects bioavailable (free, unbound) hormone, which is the fraction that enters tissues. Serum captures total hormone — useful when titrating replacement therapy where SHBG shifts matter. We often run both when symptoms don't match the labs.
I'm on hormone replacement — when should I test?
For transdermal estradiol, draw 24h after application. For oral progesterone, draw 4–6h after dose. For testosterone injections, draw at trough (just before next dose) and peak (24–48h after). We'll give you the exact timing for your protocol.
What cycle day for perimenopause testing?
Day 3 of your period for FSH / LH / E2 (ovarian reserve), and Day 19–21 for progesterone (luteal confirmation). If cycles are irregular, we draw an un-cycled panel and interpret it together with your symptom pattern.
Does birth control affect results?
Yes — oral contraceptives suppress endogenous LH / FSH / E2 and raise SHBG. Interpretation changes. Tell us what you're on and when you took the last dose.
