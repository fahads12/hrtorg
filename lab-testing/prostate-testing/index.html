Overview

Prostate testing matters most for men over 45 and for anyone considering or on testosterone replacement therapy. PSA (prostate-specific antigen) is the primary screen, but a single number without context is unreliable. We interpret PSA alongside age, prostate volume, DRE findings, free PSA ratio, and PSA velocity.

TRT does not cause prostate cancer. Current evidence does not support an increased risk of new prostate cancer on TRT. But TRT can grow an existing clinically unrecognized tumor — so we screen before starting and monitor during therapy.

When PSA is elevated or climbing, we don't jump to biopsy. The 4Kscore, PCA3, and PI-RADS MRI reduce unnecessary biopsies while catching clinically significant cancer.

FAQ

Common questions.

Does TRT cause prostate cancer?
Current evidence does not show an increased risk of new prostate cancer in men on TRT. The historical concern dates to Charles Huggins' 1941 work on castration for metastatic cancer — but that doesn't translate to TRT in men with normal baseline prostate screening. We screen before starting and monitor during therapy.
What does a rising PSA on TRT mean?
A small bump (0.3–0.5 ng/mL) in the first 3–6 months is expected as TRT normalizes prostatic activity. A sustained rise above 1.0 ng/mL or velocity >0.75/year warrants urology referral — regardless of absolute value.
Should every man get PSA screening?
USPSTF recommends shared decision-making for men 55–69 and against routine screening at 70+. We screen earlier (age 40–45) for African American men, those with family history of prostate cancer, and all men starting TRT.
