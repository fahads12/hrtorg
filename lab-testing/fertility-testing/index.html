Overview

Fertility testing serves two populations: couples actively trying to conceive and women / men who want to understand their reproductive window for family-planning timing. The workup is different for each.

For women we assess ovarian reserve (AMH, Day-3 FSH / LH / E2, antral follicle count on ultrasound), ovulation (luteal progesterone, LH surge), and uterine / tubal factors (imaging, as needed). For men we start with a semen analysis — the single most informative test — and add testosterone, FSH, LH, and prolactin if abnormal.

Fertility and HRT overlap: hormonal patterns that cause fertility issues (PCOS, premature ovarian insufficiency, hypothalamic amenorrhea, low T) are the same patterns we treat.

FAQ

Common questions.

What's a "normal" AMH for my age?
Rough reference: age 25–30: 1.5–4.0 · age 30–35: 1.0–3.5 · age 35–40: 0.8–2.5 · age 40–42: 0.4–1.5 · age 42+: <0.5. AMH predicts ovarian reserve, not fertility — a woman with low AMH can still conceive, just with fewer eggs to work with.
When should I test AMH?
Early if you're delaying childbearing (age 30+), have a family history of early menopause, had chemotherapy / radiation, or are considering egg freezing. AMH can be drawn any cycle day — it's stable.
Why semen analysis first for men?
Half of infertility is male-factor. A semen analysis is cheap, informative, and tells us whether to work up hormonal causes or move directly to assisted reproductive technology. We often repeat it after 6–8 weeks because samples vary.
