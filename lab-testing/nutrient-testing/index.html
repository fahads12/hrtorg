Overview

Deficiencies in Vitamin D, B12, folate, iron, and magnesium drive fatigue, depression, cognitive complaints, and poor exercise recovery — often misdiagnosed as hormonal or psychiatric. We screen them routinely because they're common and correction is cheap and effective.

We test functional status, not just serum levels. For B12 we add homocysteine and methylmalonic acid (MMA) — the first two markers that rise before serum B12 drops. For magnesium we use RBC magnesium, not serum (serum is tightly regulated and misses deficiency).

Nutrient status is particularly important before and during HRT or TRT. Low ferritin, Vitamin D, or thyroid cofactor minerals (selenium, zinc, iodine) can blunt treatment response.

FAQ

Common questions.

My B12 is "normal" but I feel deficient — what next?
Add methylmalonic acid (MMA) and homocysteine. These rise before serum B12 drops. If MMA is elevated with "normal" B12, it's a functional deficiency and responds to supplementation.
Why RBC magnesium instead of serum?
Serum magnesium is tightly regulated — the body pulls from bone and muscle to maintain it. Serum stays normal until deficiency is severe. RBC magnesium reflects intracellular status and catches deficiency earlier.
What's my Vitamin D target?
For symptomatic correction, 50–80 ng/mL. Below 30 is deficient; 30–50 is suboptimal. Supplementation of 2,000–5,000 IU daily is usually safe; we recheck at 10–12 weeks.
