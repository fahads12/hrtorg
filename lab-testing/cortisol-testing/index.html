Overview

Cortisol follows a daily rhythm: high at wake (~6–8am), dropping across the day, lowest at midnight. A single serum draw tells you the number at one moment; it doesn't tell you the rhythm. For functional complaints — morning fatigue, mid-afternoon crash, evening wired-tired — we use a four-point salivary cortisol collected across the day.

DHEA-S is the paired adrenal marker — the slow buffer that counterbalances cortisol. A high-cortisol / low-DHEA pattern is the classic chronic-stress signature. A flat cortisol curve (no AM rise) is HPA axis dysregulation.

We don't use the term "adrenal fatigue" clinically — the adrenals rarely stop producing. We call it HPA-axis dysregulation and treat the drivers (sleep, inflammation, chronic stress, blood sugar instability) alongside targeted supplementation.

FAQ

Common questions.

What is HPA-axis dysregulation?
The hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis loses its normal rhythm — the morning cortisol surge flattens, afternoon doesn't drop properly, nighttime stays elevated. It drives the "tired-but-wired" presentation.
Why saliva for cortisol?
Saliva reflects free (unbound, bioactive) cortisol. Serum captures total cortisol which is mostly bound to CBG and not bioavailable. For diurnal pattern analysis, saliva is the clinical standard.
Is hydrocortisone replacement ever appropriate?
Only for diagnosed Addison's disease, pituitary insufficiency, or iatrogenic suppression — not for functional HPA dysregulation. For functional cases we use lifestyle, adaptogens, sleep protocol, and cortisol-timing.
