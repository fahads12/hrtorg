hs-CRP, ESR, IL-6, ferritin, and fibrinogen — the systemic markers that catch chronic low-grade inflammation driving disease.

Overview

Chronic low-grade inflammation is the final common pathway for cardiovascular disease, diabetes, Alzheimer's, autoimmune conditions, depression, and most age-related illness. Unlike acute inflammation (which hurts and goes away), chronic inflammation is silent and corrosive.

We screen with hs-CRP as the first marker. If elevated, we add ESR, IL-6, ferritin (acute phase), and fibrinogen to look for drivers — autoimmunity, hidden infection, insulin resistance, periodontal disease, or stress-driven HPA activation.

Elevated inflammation also blunts response to hormone therapy — it raises SHBG, suppresses T3 conversion, and worsens insulin resistance. Correcting it is often the fastest path to feeling better.

FAQ

Common questions.

My hs-CRP is elevated — what causes it?
In order of frequency: visceral adiposity / insulin resistance (treat with weight loss, exercise, GLP-1), periodontal disease (dental workup), chronic infection (sinus, UTI, dental), autoimmune conditions, sleep deprivation, and chronic stress. We work through the differential.
How do I lower chronic inflammation?
Weight loss (if visceral adiposity), strength training 3x/week, 7+ hours of sleep, Mediterranean-pattern diet, omega-3 supplementation, treating hidden sources (dental, GI, hormonal). Pharmacotherapy for diagnosed conditions.
Is a hs-CRP of 2 "normal"?
The lab reports it as low-risk, but 2 is firmly in the mid-tier of cardiovascular risk — about double the risk of someone at <1. We treat <1 as the target.
