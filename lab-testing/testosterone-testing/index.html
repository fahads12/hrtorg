Testosterone Testing

Total, free, and bioavailable testosterone, SHBG, and estradiol help determine TRT eligibility, dose, and safety.

Overview

Testosterone circulates in three fractions: tightly bound to SHBG (unavailable), loosely bound to albumin (bioavailable), and free (active). Total testosterone alone misses the picture — a man with normal total T but high SHBG can have symptomatic hypogonadism from low free T.

For men, we draw total + free testosterone, SHBG, estradiol (sensitive assay), LH, FSH, PSA, hematocrit, and prolactin. The result tells us whether low T is primary (testicular) or secondary (pituitary / hypothalamic) — which changes treatment.

For women, testosterone drives libido, muscle mass, cognition, and energy. The range is small (15–70 ng/dL total) so timing and assay choice matter. We use LC-MS/MS for female testosterone, not immunoassay.

For both sexes, re-testing on TRT is scheduled around dose timing: trough for injections (just before next shot), 24h after transdermal dose, 2 weeks after pellet insertion.

FAQ

Common questions.

What total testosterone level qualifies for TRT?
Most guidelines use <300 ng/dL with symptoms. We treat the symptom pattern alongside the number — a man at 350 with crushing fatigue, erectile dysfunction, and low free T on labs is a candidate.
Why also draw estradiol in men?
Testosterone aromatizes to estradiol. On TRT, E2 can climb and cause side effects (water retention, gynecomastia, mood). Over-suppression with aromatase inhibitors is worse — crashed E2 causes joint pain, mood collapse, and bone loss. We aim for E2 in the 20–40 pg/mL range.
When should I test on TRT?
For injections: trough draw (just before next dose) tells us the floor; peak draw (24–48h after) tells us whether we're overshooting. For transdermal: 2–4 hours after application. For pellets: 4 weeks after insertion.
Will TRT shut down my fertility?
Yes — exogenous testosterone suppresses LH / FSH and sperm production. If you want to preserve fertility, we use clomiphene or hCG-maintained TRT instead. See our TRT-safe fertility page.
